## 三、英文材料

**Title**:

```
Making Original Texts into AI-Callable Knowledge Assets: A Reproducible Method and Evaluation
```

**Subtitle**:

```
With One Original Text and Three Public-Domain Classics as Cases
```

**Author**:

```
Meta-consciousness Artificial Intelligence Technology Co., Ltd., Chongqing (MCAI, Meta-consciousness AI Team)
```

**Abstract** (about 200 words):

```
Large language models are changing how humanities texts are accessed, but they often misread original texts: guessing from literal meanings, substituting homonymous concepts, or producing plausible passages that cannot be traced back to the source. This paper presents a method for turning original texts into machine-callable knowledge assets through six stages: corpus preparation, term extraction and adjudication, definition layering, question-answer writing, relation extraction, and citation annotation with verification. The method treats provenance as a first-class field: every definition and quotation is annotated with source document, chapter, character span, and edition, and verbatim verification serves as the release threshold. Entries without provenance are marked as missing, and the system explicitly refuses to answer when evidence is absent. We implemented the method on one original text and three public-domain classics. The main knowledge base contains 64 terms, 122 question-answer pairs, 270 relations, and 213 citations. The three classics yielded 10, 13, and 12 concepts respectively, with a 100% citation verification rate. The replication exposed three rule gaps: single-character terms, Chinese numeral numbering, and texts without explicit definition sentences. The contribution is a reproducible, verifiable workflow with public field specifications and an evaluation benchmark for knowledge assetization in the digital humanities.
```

**Keywords**:

```
digital humanities; knowledge assets; citation verification; knowledge graph; large language models; reproducibility
```
